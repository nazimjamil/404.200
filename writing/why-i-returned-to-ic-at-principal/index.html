Why I returned to IC at Principal

CAREER FEB 2026 6 MIN

Eighteen months ago I turned down a Director of Engineering promotion. This is the first time I have written about it publicly.

The offer

The offer was generous. Larger team. Expanded scope. The title upgrade that most Principal Engineers treat as the logical next step. My manager framed it well: “You’ve already been doing the work. This makes it official.”

She was right that I had been doing some of the work. The cross-team alignment. The roadmap shaping. The occasional skip-level conversation when something was going sideways. But “some of the work” is not the same as “the job.”

What I would have lost

The job of a Director of Engineering is fundamentally about people systems: hiring pipelines, performance cycles, team health, org design. These are important. They are also not where I generate my highest leverage.

My highest-leverage contribution is architectural. It is sitting inside a hard technical problem with a whiteboard and a few of the right engineers, and making a decision that will compound for three years. It is the GraphQL schema that other teams build on. It is the reliability model that becomes the standard.

That work requires depth. Directors trade depth for breadth. The swap is rational for the organisation. It is not rational for me.

The return

Returning to IC at Principal is not a retreat. It is a bet on compounding. Architectural decisions compound. Platform quality compounds. The systems I have shaped at Expedia will still be running after I am gone — and that is a more durable kind of leverage than any org chart.

I am not anti-management. I am pro-craft. The distinction matters.
