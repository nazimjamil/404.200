Observability is a product surface

ESSAY MAY 2025 7 MIN

Observability is not a dashboard you add after launch. It is not a Grafana board that someone built in year two. Observability is a product surface — designed, with users, with use cases, with a feedback loop.

Who are the users?

The users of your observability surface are engineers. On-call engineers at 3am, specifically. They are trying to answer a question under time pressure: what is wrong, where is it, and what do I do about it?

If your observability surface cannot answer that question in under five minutes, it is not fit for purpose. Not because the data is absent — it probably is not — but because it has not been designed for the use case.

The three questions

Every observability surface should be able to answer three questions about any system, at any time:

  1. Is it working? (The SLO dashboard. Binary. Red or green.)
  2. Why is it broken? (Traces, spans, structured logs. The path from symptom to cause.)
  3. What changed? (Deployment markers. Config changes. The diff between now and the last time it worked.)

These are product requirements. They should be designed for, not discovered.

The feedback loop

The on-call rotation is your user research. Every time an engineer is woken up and spends twenty minutes navigating dashboards to find the root cause, that is a usability failure. Treat it as one.

Run a post-incident review. Ask not just “why did the system fail” but “why did the observability surface not surface the problem faster.” The answer will improve your next deployment more than any alert threshold tuning.

Observability is a product. Ship it accordingly.
