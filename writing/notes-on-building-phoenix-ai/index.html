Notes on building Phoenix.ai

CASE STUDY OCT 2024 14 MIN

Phoenix.ai is Expedia Group’s flagship internal AI product. I was the principal architect. These are my notes on building it.

The brief

The brief was deliberately open: build an AI system that helps Expedia’s internal teams work more effectively. That openness was both a gift and a trap.

The gift: design latitude. We could choose the right problem rather than the assigned one.

The trap: infinite scope. Without constraints, an AI product becomes a feature list. We needed to find the right constraint.

Finding the constraint

We spent the first six weeks in discovery — talking to teams, watching workflows, looking for the pattern where human judgment was being applied to a task that a system could handle better.

The pattern we found was in content operations: teams reviewing, classifying, and routing large volumes of structured content with repetitive judgment patterns. High volume. Low variance. High cost of errors.

This was the constraint. Not “AI for Expedia” but “AI for content operations.”

The architecture

Phoenix.ai is a multi-agent system with a human-in-the-loop escalation path. The core components:

Classifier agents — narrow, fine-tuned models for specific content categories. Fast, cheap, high accuracy on known patterns.

Router — orchestrates classifier output and determines routing: auto-process, review queue, or human escalation.

Review surface — the human-in-the-loop UI. Designed for speed: reviewers see the agent’s recommendation, confidence score, and reasoning. One-click approval or override.

Feedback pipeline — overrides are captured and fed back into classifier training. The system gets better with use.

What we got wrong

We underestimated the importance of the review surface. Our first version was functional but slow. Reviewers spent more time in it than with manual processing. The AI had added a step, not removed one.

We rebuilt the review surface in sprint four. Faster interaction model. Better confidence display. The time savings turned positive.

The lesson: the human-in-the-loop is a product surface. Design it first.

Results

Phoenix.ai is now processing at scale. The measurable outcomes are not mine to publish, but the direction is clear: significant reduction in manual review time, improved consistency, positive reviewer feedback.

The architecture is holding. The feedback loop is working.
